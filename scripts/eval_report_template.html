Coil Map Benchmark

Eight ways to estimate receive-coil sensitivities, compared on identical calibration data across synthetic phantoms with known ground truth, a fully sampled 15-channel knee and a prospectively undersampled 8-channel brain. Every map set is scored on how well it explains the measured k-space after combining and re-expanding, on the quality and SNR of the combined reference image, on SENSE reconstructions at R = 2–6, and on the maps themselves.

3-D encoding

Volumetric data

The two full-wave cases test the wave-aware (Helmholtz) model: fields that obey Maxwell's equations in conductive tissue, not the quasi-static Biot–Savart law. 3-D Cartesian k-space, 3-D FFTs, a 12³ calibration block for every method, the field and its Laplace penalty fitted through the whole volume. SENSE uses 2-D undersampling over the two phase-encoding axes (R₁ × R₂, plus the ACS block).

Definitions

Metrics

Combine → expand residual / noise floor
Coil images I from the acquired k-space are combined with the maps (per-pixel least squares, m = Cᴴ I / Cᴴ C), re-expanded to C·m and transformed back. The residual energy at the acquired samples is divided by the residual that perfect maps would leave, which is pure noise and is computed exactly by passing whitened noise through the same combine/expand (≈ (N−1)σ² per sample). 1.00 = indistinguishable from the true maps. Reported separately outside the ACS block, which no estimator saw (a held-out test), and inside it, where values below 1 mean the maps absorbed noise.
Radial residual profile
The same ratio in rings of |k|, showing at which spatial frequencies the maps fail to explain the data.
SNR efficiency ↑
Noise-debiased |ĉᴴ i| / ‖i‖ per pixel for RSS-normalised maps ĉ: the fraction of the optimal (Roemer) combined SNR that the reference image reaches. Equals 1 for the true maps; averaged over the object.
Reference image NRMSE
Magnitude of the fully sampled SENSE-1 combination with normalised maps against |ρ|‖c_true‖ (synthetic) or the RSS image (real), after one least-squares scale, within the object.
SENSE at R
Uniform 1-D undersampling along phase encoding plus the ACS lines, Tikhonov CG-SENSE (λ = 10⁻³, 40 iterations) with normalised maps. “Common support”: all methods solve only inside the same dilated object mask, so background map values cannot help or hurt conditioning; “as delivered” uses the maps as returned. Image NRMSE / SSIM against the method-independent reference; missing-k NRMSE compares the predicted k-space at the dropped lines with the measured data (noise floor shown in the tooltip).
g-factor
Analytic SENSE g-factor for uniform R = 4 from the normalised maps (mean over the object). It is the noise amplification of an unregularised recon with these maps; low values are only good if the unfolding is also correct.
Held-out k-space (prospective)
Brain only: 20 % of the acquired samples outside the ACS are withheld (two folds), CG-SENSE on the rest, NRMSE of the predicted withheld samples; noise floor = √(N σ² · #samples)/‖y‖.
Ground-truth map errors
Subspace error 1 − |ĉᴴ ĉ_true|² (gauge-free); relative-phase error after removing the per-pixel common phase, |c|²-weighted, in degrees; magnitude error of the normalised maps.
Vortices, roughness, coverage
Phase singularities of the maps as delivered inside the object; gauge-invariant roughness = mean ‖P(r) − P(r+1)‖_F of the projector P = ĉĉᴴ between neighbouring pixels; coverage = fraction of the object where the maps are non-zero (ESPIRiT crops).
Protocol

How it was run, and its limits

  • All data are pre-whitened (knee: covariance from signal-free background; brain: from the k-space periphery; synthetic: white by construction), so noise floors are exact.
  • Calibration-only protocol: on the knee and synthetic cases every estimator receives exactly the same ACS block, zero elsewhere. Classical baselines use their standard settings: ESPIRiT = BART ecalib -m1 with its default eigenvalue crop, NLINV = BART ncalib, Walsh and Inati with a smoothing window of one low-resolution pixel.
  • Biot–Savart fits: 1000 wire + 1500 PINN iterations, λphys = 0.1, calibration modelled on a 2× oversampled grid. One run each; no tuning per dataset.
  • The knee FOV cuts through the leg along the readout, so the object touches the top and bottom edge. FFT-based estimators wrap structure across that edge; this affects every method and is visible in the last rows of the error maps.
  • Synthetic truth is a Biot–Savart field, which favours the physics model; the mismatch case removes that advantage. The knee is the decisive real test because its fully sampled data give a reference for every metric.
  • Runtimes are single-threaded CPU and include BART process start-up for ESPIRiT and NLINV.
